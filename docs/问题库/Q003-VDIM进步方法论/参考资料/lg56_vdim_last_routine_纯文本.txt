In the simplest of terms, aiming refers to  being able to move your crosshair from point   A to point B in the most efficient way possible.  In other words, it means being able to control   your mouse exactly how you want it to move. If we go a little deeper, all the motions and   techniques required to do this can be classified  into three main categories: Clicking, Tracking,   and Target Switching. If you want to be even  more specific, you can further divide these   into subcategories that mostly relate to the way  targets move and behave within each main category,   as well as the specific mouse motions  you need to perform to excel at them.  Now, even though you can theoretically divide  aiming into different categories—and different   shooter games usually cater more to one or two  of these at a time, such as Clicking in games   like VALORANT, Tracking in Overwatch, and Target  Switching in Call of Duty—at the end of the day,   aiming is one individual motor  skill. These categories simply   act as building blocks to form your true aim. This means that even if you think your game of   choice doesn’t feature much Tracking, for example,  practicing that aiming technique will still help   you improve your mouse control and achieve new  levels of consistency and confidence in your aim   that might otherwise take much longer to develop. Finally, an integral part of any training routine   is having the ability to properly track  your improvement, which is what makes   this training method so special! For this,  we’ll use the Voltaic Benchmarks to measure   your progress through all the different aiming  categories and subcategories. Plus, it provides   the additional fun incentive of earning a  Voltaic Rank to show off your aiming skills!  I am excited to announce the Voltaic Daily  Improvement Method—a week-long training   regimen that covers all the different aiming  techniques in the most complete way possible,   while also helping you train to achieve  higher ranks in the Voltaic Benchmarks!  The way this method works is by dedicating  two days of the week to each main category.   This means that on Mondays and Tuesdays,  you cover all the Clicking subcategories;   on Wednesdays and Thursdays, you cover all  Tracking subcategories; and on Fridays and   Saturdays, you train all the Target Switching  subcategories. Finally, on Sunday, you have   the option to play your weakest category once  again, grind a specific scenario where you feel   close to getting a new high score, or simply rest! On each of these days, you’ll find a combination   of hand-picked scenarios to train all  the fundamental skills of each category,   as well as variations of the Voltaic Benchmarks  that will help you understand and train for each   of them—and ultimately beat your high scores. Of  course, the respective benchmarks of each skill   are also included in the routines themselves,  which means that simply by playing the routines,   you are actively benchmarking your  aim and tracking your progress.  Additionally, besides being separated  by aiming category and day of the week,   the routines are also divided into  five tiers of difficulty. This way,   anyone—from people who are completely new to  aiming on mouse and keyboard to the best aimers   in the world—can find value in these routines.
