On the surface, having great tracking aim seems much harder than actually is. The abundance of visual effects, recoil, and movement in modern FPS games creates this illusion of difficulty surrounding the skill. In this video, we will show you how to use a simpler approach to tracking to achieve incredible results. When you strip all of the art and effects away, tracking a target in game comes down to being able to trace some simple shapes with your crosshair. For example, a target running in a straight line, AD strafing back and forth, tracking a jumping target, a falling target, flying through the air, and both moving in the same direction, you don't even have to aim. I think we can agree that tracing these lines isn't very difficult. So, the question is, how do we apply this in game? Firstly, we need to experience a shift in perspective. In the chaos of modern FPS gameplay, enemies may appear to be moving erratically, but instead of becoming flustered and wildly tracking them, stay calm and identify what line you need to draw. Strip all of that excess information away and focus on the fundamentals. This step is helped greatly by learning to focus on your target instead of looking at them. There's a big difference between the two. For example, when you look at the ceiling fan, you can see the blades, but they are a blur. So, if you focus on a single blade, you can perfectly follow it spinning around. This is the difference we are looking for. When you focus on a target, they will appear to slow down, and most importantly, you are now able to absorb visual cues that will indicate where they are moving. If you can focus on a target and understand their intentions, tracing these lines becomes much easier. You might prepare yourself to track an AD strafe, or if you caught them out in the open, you can prepare for a straight line that they will move into reach the closest cover. The second step to applying this in game is to smooth out your aim. For example, when tracking an AD strafing target, you don't want to trace the lines by flicking back and forth. You want to be drawing the line at the same speed. The key to smooth tracking is being able to match your crosshair speed to the speed your target is moving on screen. A quick method to practice this skill and to get a feel for tracing lines is to play smoothness trainer 60 in Kovak. The target has a predetermined strafing pattern, so there's no need for reactivity, and when you can track this target with your crosshair rarely falling off, start playing same strafe smoothness. The targets follow the same strafing pattern, but progressively get thinner and faster. You will want to be adept at matching any enemy's movement, rather than only being comfortable tracing at one speed. To further improve smoothness, we have a video dedicated to improving jittery and shaky aim that you can check out in the description. To summarize, anyone can greatly improve their tracking by recognizing that enemy movement is much simpler and more predictable than it may appear. By focusing on a target, you can slow things down and understand their intentions, allowing you to trace simple shapes with your crosshair. Tracking targets smoothly with a deliberate speed is better than fast jerky movements. While this only scratches the surface of tracking aim, this will provide an excellent foundation for future growth. We hope you found this video helpful.
