But lastly, let's move onto the last type of peek that we're going to talk about, which is peeking through smokes. Peeking through smokes can be really dangerous, but there are some general rules to follow when doing it. Basically the first rule is that you should almost never peek out of the front of a smoke. This is why having your smokes flushed with the doorway is so important, because it gives your team the best odds to punish players for pushing through them. Instead, when peeking through a smoke, you want to peek through either the side of the smoke, or even better, the back of the smoke. The goal here being to get out of the smoke, as soon as possible before your gun fight. By strafing out of the smoke you hide your gun for as long as you can before taking your duel. And by exiting the smoke from the back, you'll actually use the smoke as cover so you can take a duel without being hindered by the smoke at all. This rule should be applied, whenever you exit from any smoke in the game. Something we need to cover next though is probably one of the best types of peeks you can pull off, and that is a double peek. Double peeking is quite simply, when you peek with a teammate. Two of you peek the same angle, in an attempt to take a 2v1 This can be really great if you think someone is holding an angle, or in a specific position, that way you and your teammate can double peek him, and at the very worst, trade out the kill. To do this, generally what you'll do to avoid catastrophe is have one player, generally the one in the back swing wide, and the other one will swing close. This way you'll avoid getting collated, and give your team the best odds of coming on top. Normally what will happen is the player you're swinging will try to track the person who is wide swinging, and it will result in them missing both targets, and lose the fight. This tactic is incredibly common in higher level play, and is the easiest way to assure your team success. Starting out with the Wide Swing. This is probably the most common type of peek, because it's psychologically, it makes the most sense. You want to swing around a corner and look for a fight, so you swing wide, and scan for the enemy as you do this. However, in many situations, wide swinging can actually be bad and here's why. If you're wide swinging with 0 information of what is on the other side, you're often times putting yourself in a very dangerous scenario. This is because the enemies know where you can come from, but you have no idea where the enemies are. Performing a wide swing with 0 information, and using 0 utility, is what we call a "Dry Peek", and is generally a pretty bad idea. You could be swinging into an Operator, maybe an enemy is playing an off angle that will be difficult to flick to, maybe there are 3 enemies on the other side, you don't know, that's the problem. This is why, if you're wide swinging an angle, it's important that you have some sort of idea of what to expect on the other side. Other things to note with wide swings, is that the closer you are to a wall, the faster your wide swing is generally going to be. This is why it's important to try and close the gap against operator users, because it's going to be a lot more easier for them to land this shot Versus Landing this shot Moving onto something similar to a Wide Swing, and this is what people call a Ferrari Peek I've heard this term used a lot, I'm not sure if this the common terminology, but this is basically when someone takes a Wide Swing to the extreme. This can be really strong because players will expect you to stop, and will try to flick towards your head, but instead of stopping, you will keep going, and make it very hard for them to land their shot. This is especially strong when abusing run and gun on weapons like the Frenzy, the Spectre, or the Judge. Definitely not the most precise way to take down your opponents, but still good to use at times. Moving over towards the Short Peek though. This peek is basically what you use when you refer to that slice the pie method a lot of videos have mentioned. You'll generally use this peek when you're unsure of where the enemy is, but you aren't able to use utility to clear them out. You'll slowly clear each individual off angle, until you're able to locate the enemy, and land your shot. Generally, you'll want to perform this peek, from as far away from the wall as possible, as we mentioned this is a slow clear. Moving onto more of our Info related peeks, let's talk about Jiggle Peeking and Jump Peeking next. Jiggle peeking often referred to as shoulder peeking, is generally something you'll use either to gain info, or to bait out a shot from your enemies. Sometimes it can even just be used to get the enemies attention. "HEY LOOK AT ME" When doing jiggle peeking correctly, you shouldn't really be able to see anything on the other side of the wall. This peek is most often performed with your knife out, and standing close to the wall so you can peek as fast as possible. You'll just barely show your shoulder, and often times this is enough to bait out an operator shot. Once they've taken their shot, you can now pick up the angle yourself, so that if they repeek, you'll have the advantage. You can also use this opportunity to move to a location that isn't exactly on the angle, to give yourself an even greater advantage. When performing this peek vs someone who isn't using an Operator though, it can also be useful to mess with their recoil, or crosshair placement. Oftentimes players will try to spray at the corner, or prefire you when they see this peek, and you can use this opportunity to combine it with a wide swing, and pick up your well deserved kill. Now you might think, if I can't see a player, what do I do if they don't shoot at me? Well in case you're specifically looking for info, rather than going wider and getting yourself killed, a much safer peek you can pull off is called a Jump peek. Jump peeking is great for gathering info because it makes it a lot more difficult to headshot you. When peeking wide, your still can run the risk of getting headshot, but with a jump peek, it's very unlikely to get punished too hard for this when done right. The most you'll normally expect is a body shot. To perform a jump peek, approach the angle, and jump forward, and then immediately hold the S and A key, or S and D key depending on where you're peeking from. This is one way to perform it, the other way is to just swipe your mouse across the screen to air strafe out of view, both of these work fine. I personally feel the air strafe is better, but it's your call. I generally will jump peek on agents who don't have info gathering abilities, and when I'm not intending to take a fight.
